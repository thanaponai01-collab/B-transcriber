# HKS (โหนกระแส) principles (the why)

The show's own principles. Read [the main principles](../PRINCIPLES.md) first; where this file
departs from them, this file wins for HKS.

**Source of truth: [registry/](registry/README.md)**, one file per rule or principle; a new one goes there first. This file keeps the why in prose.

**IDs:** `HKS-<n>`. Rules ([rules.md](rules.md)) and code cite these IDs.

## Principles

The first confirmed principles are HKS-C1 to C12 (2026-11-24). The HKS work before that recorded
**rules** (how the user cuts) rather than the reasons. The next time the user edits or answers questions, ask the why behind each rule in
[rules.md](rules.md) and record it here: the principle, then *(confirmed / seen / guess)* and the
user's words.

Already shared with 1RaceClub and moved to the main file: **each point is told once** (M-4).

**Two programs (the user's word, 2026-11-24).** โหนกระแส is the main show, cut from the raw broadcast.
โหนหลังกระแส is a compilation of usually 4 already-edited โหนกระแส long videos, cut down again. So rules read
from the dailies are about cutting a broadcast, and rules read from EP.162/167 (HKS-18 on) are about re-cutting
finished cuts: a compilation's "kept share" (19–23 %) is of an already-cut video, and its longer pieces (median
8–10 s) can be longer because each source was already tightened once (inference, not yet asked).

## Confirmed by the user (2026-11-24, answers to the overnight study's questions)

Each is **confirmed**; the user's own words are quoted where they added to the offered answer.

- **HKS-C1. No jump cuts in the studio picture** (rule 5): viewers notice them **and** the show should
  look like the broadcast. Both reasons.
- **HKS-C2. Cover only with footage from the episode itself** (rule 6): it keeps the story honest (the
  picture is this case's real evidence) **and** avoids copyright claims.
- **HKS-C3. The TV clock is blurred to hide that it is a re-cut**: a clock that jumps between pieces shows
  the edit. So the clock and the 3HD bug are **fine on studio frames** (seen 1123, 1110); cover them where
  they would show the cut.
- **HKS-C4. The host's on-air intro and recap are cut** (HKS-22) because the hook does the intro's job and
  a recap repeats what the viewer just saw, **and** for length.
- **HKS-C5. Viewer comments and the host's warm-up opinions are cut** because they slow the story **and**
  for neutrality. Exception: a viewer comment that is the objection a guest or the host then answers.
- **HKS-C6. Ask, then tell is an allowed opening** (1110, 1117): the story may open on the host question
  that sets up the guest's first story sentence.
- **HKS-C7. The host stays when he carries the story** (HKS-8 confirmed, rule 3 rewritten): the chapter
  question, the challenge the viewer wants asked, the clearest summary, the verdict, legal cover. Cut his
  warm-up, clarifying questions, name checks, filler and most jokes.
- **HKS-C8. Law answers the guest's own case, never lectures** (HKS-12 confirmed, rule 2 rewritten): right
  after the fact it explains, or as the verdict. The user: "sometimes it could be somewhere at around
  ending like every story tells, then law answers": the whole story first, then the law as its answer.
- **HKS-C9. A cover has no fixed minimum length** (replaces "covers ≥ 4 s"; HKS-21 confirmed): it lasts as
  long as the words that point at it, and sits on the footage at the exact moment the speaker talks about
  it. The user: the broadcast "mostly split[s] the screen when they have evidence or b-roll. So the split
  screen, that's where you can use b-roll and to cover the cut as long as it's in a topic or at the real
  broadcast showing it." So: look for the broadcast's split screens first; that footage may cover a cut
  when it is on the topic, or when the broadcast was showing it at that moment.
- **HKS-C10. Film damage goes on B-roll that is zoomed to full screen** (rule 6 narrowed; the user: "for the
  B-roll things that is zoomed to full screen"). The study's exceptions (HKS-17 note: 1123's CCTV, 1012's
  body covers without it, 1117's screenshots with it) are not yet re-checked against "zoomed full screen".
- **HKS-C11. Headline cards mainly pay off in the body**; a card may tease beyond it, but the default is a
  payoff (so the "every headline card is paid off" check below stays a check, not an absolute rule).
- **HKS-C12. A compilation plays its cases in source order** (EP.168 question; HKS-18). The user downloads
  each source from YouTube and numbers the file: play them by that number. With no number, by the date the
  video was posted on YouTube. A story told across several videos stays in its own order.

## Agreed improvements (agent proposed, user agreed 2026-11-24)

Proposed by the agent from gaps in the user's own cuts; the user agreed to each. **confirmed** as the way
forward, not as a description of past cuts. The checks are in [rules.md](rules.md) ("Checks before handover").

- **HKS-C13. Every promise is kept.** Whatever the hook, a headline card or the host raises is answered in
  the cut, or left open on purpose. Why: an unanswered question reads as a mistake. Misses seen: 1020 "is it
  real?", 1110 WatchDog (answered 15 min later), 1012 the money never repaid, 1117 premise not said until 5:27.
- **HKS-C14. The middle is re-hooked.** No stretch of about 3–4 min without a new picture, a host question
  or a turn. A turn said aloud counts; one picture held a long time is fine (user, 2026-11-24, on 1123's
  3:20 single shot: "it would be fine"). Why: that is where viewers drop off (1120 8–14 min, 1123 7–10 min ran 4–6 min with none).
- **HKS-C15. Each side's strongest line is in.** Before locking, name each side's single strongest line and
  check it is kept. Why: they were cut on 1003 ("20,000 a month, a bucket"), 1117 ("if only I'd picked
  up") and 1120 (the witness's points for the guest).
- **HKS-C16. An open ending says it is open.** If the story is unresolved, end on a line that says so (a
  threat, a pending case, a follow-up), not on a cut that just stops (1002, 1003, 1012).
- **HKS-C17. Tighten at speaker changes first** (HKS-27 confirmed). Dropping another speaker's restatement
  lands on the broadcast's own camera switch, so no cover is needed; tighten inside one speaker's run only
  under split-screen cover (HKS-C9).
- **HKS-C18. Watch time counts, but partly** (the main file's watch-time candidate, for HKS). Each second is
  judged by whether it keeps the viewer watching, **after** honesty and fairness: the accused's answer
  (HKS-6), legal cover and the facts that keep the story true stay even when slow.
- **HKS-C19. Challenge the user's cuts** on three things (the user: hooks "maybe there's something stronger,
  harder, or more dramatic"; the pace, "too long for the whole video, like keeps explaining something, or
  the footage, B-roll"; and the cuts, "jump cut or snappy cut … when the [broadcast] switch camera, is it
  cut off"). In every draft, say where the agent would hook harder, where an explanation or a B-roll piece
  runs long, and where a cut lands snappy or clips a word at a camera switch.

- **HKS-C20. A flicker is a jump; a short shot is not** (user, 2026-11-24, on 1123's 0.7–0.9 s shots after a
  cut: "it's just flow through story, not just snap like flickering… It's there first, then got switch off").
  The "no snappy scene changes" rule is about a shot too short to read (v4: 0.16 s), not any shot under 1 s.
- **HKS-C21. When a guest accuses, the other side's rebuttal stays** if the broadcast has it (user,
  2026-11-24, 1123: the dead man's family say no gun was thrown). Narrows HKS-6 to a rule.
- **HKS-C22. A fact the picture shows need not be said** (user, 2026-11-24: "the evidence show on the
  B-roll"). The check is then that the picture really shows it: in 1123 the graphic marks the boss
  "ออกหมายจับ", not "จับแล้ว".
- **HKS-C23. A punchline needs its set-up.** (HKS-28 confirmed; user, 2026-11-24: "all confirmed") The agent's hook ended on the bet's payoff alone ("ผมโกนรอเลยพี่");
  the user put its condition in front ("500 ล้านพี่หนุ่มเปิดผมตาม"). A cold viewer can't laugh at a bet they
  haven't heard. This is HKS-24 the other way round, and the same idea as ask-then-tell (HKS-C6).
- **HKS-C24. The body doesn't replay a hook clip in the middle; the ending may, as the payoff.** (HKS-29 confirmed; user, 2026-11-24: "all confirmed") The user took
  the MV jab ("อย่าพูด", already in the hook) out of the FB body and put the My Way piece there instead,
  but kept the bet at the end, with more of it than the hook shows. The hook promises; the ending pays.
- **HKS-C25. Every shot is framed on its own.** (HKS-30 confirmed; user, 2026-11-24: "all confirmed") The user re-zoomed the agent's trailer shots one by one
  (263 → 280–296, the MV cutaway 263 → 338) and, in the square, cut a wide cover in two and moved the
  second half to follow the subject. One zoom for a whole set of shots is a preset (M-1), not framing.
- **HKS-C26. A short ends on a held face.** (HKS-31 confirmed; user, 2026-11-24: "all confirmed") The user's 1-min ends on the last speaker's face held under
  the sting (0.5 s of source stretched over the 2.84 s sting, zoomed in 187 %), with glitch flashes and
  a whoosh, then the end card. The last thing the viewer sees is a reaction, not the logo.

## Candidates from the overnight study (2026-11-23, none confirmed)

Read from the user's finished YT cuts of 1001, 1003, 1012, 1020, 1110, 1112, 1117, 1120, 1123 and the

- **HKS-1. One length, any story** (seen in all 12; held on the holdouts). 17–20 min whatever the
  broadcast length. Why: the channel's format; the budget decides how hard each episode is cut.
- **HKS-2. The hook is a promise the body plays out** (seen in all 9; held). 18–27 s; its lines come
  back in the body (1003, 1112, 1117, 1123). Which lines the user picks is the least predictable
  part (holdouts: 1–2 of 5 right).
  *1109:* 13 of 14 hook pieces play again in the body; the one that does not opens a verdict the body cuts.
- **HKS-3. Story days have one proven spine** (seen 1012, 1112, 1120, 1001; narrowed by the holdouts:
  issue days and panels keep every voice, thinned; see HKS-11). Why: a thread with proof can't be
  argued away.
- **HKS-4. The first clear telling wins; a recap keeps only what's new** (seen 1001, 1020, 1112, 1120;
  1118 holdout). A host one-liner can replace a guest's slow version.
- **HKS-5. Broadcast order unless the viewer needs it elsewhere** (0–4 moves per cut; held). Moves seen:
  a fact answered where it's asked (1123, 1003), evidence before a denial (1003), a scattered thread
  gathered (1110, 1020). The single move is hard to predict (holdouts: 0 of 3).
- **HKS-6. The accused gets to answer, at length** (seen 1003, 1020, 1120, 167; not tested on the
  holdouts). Exception: 1003 cut the son's side. Why: fairness, and legal safety.
- **HKS-7. Cut hard under a picture that proves the words; elsewhere keep pieces long** (seen in
  all 9). **Frame-checked on all 12 YT cuts** ([study/joins.md](study/joins.md)): of 345 uncovered joins,
  311 land on a camera change and 9 on a layout or inset change. 5 sit under a still (map, chat) where
  nothing moves. Only 2 are real same-shot jumps: 1120 686.6 inside an insert clip, and 1123 1178.9 on
  the masked three-shot (0.2 s). Why: no visible jump, and proof on screen when a claim is made.
- **HKS-8. The host stays as the questioner** (seen in all 9; held), narrowing rule 3. The chapter
  question, the challenge, the clearest summary stay. **Confirmed as HKS-C7** (2026-11-24), verdict
  included (1109 cut it; guess: the host was himself a party there, HKS-13).
- **HKS-9 → see HKS-12.** (Was "law follows the fact it explains"; the holdouts showed the test is
  whether it answers a guest's own case, not where it sits.)
- **HKS-10. The ending closes the lead thread** (narrowed: the hook-echo ending is seen only in 1112
  and 1117; the holdouts ended on the lead guest's thread or the answer to the body's last question,
  1109, 1118). The mood varies: reassurance, resolve, outrage, threat.
- **HKS-11. The day's type sets the shape** (guess, from holdout 1002; fits 1001, 1110, 1118). Story
  day: one spine. Issue day: a briefing (the damage, each voice's best beat, the takeaways, the
  threat). Panel: every guest, thinned.
  Advice day (seen 20260929, flooded cars): broadcast order with one move, the experts trimmed
  word by word, the key tip repeated on purpose (M-4 "each point once" does not apply), the hook
  is the most counter-intuitive tip, and the end is the human cost ([study/20260929-yt.md](study/20260929-yt.md)).
- **HKS-12. An expert answers a case, never lectures** (seen 1109, 1118, 1123; 1120 dropped the
  Supreme Court lecture). Law or expert talk stays only as the answer to a question about a
  guest's own case ("what happens to me now?"), even when long.
  *Full study of 1118 (2026-11-24):* a fact given twice on air is kept where it answers a guest's own case (the 1599 hotline, kept as the ending at t 1067.2) and cut where it answered a general question, even if that telling came first: HKS-12 overrides HKS-4. Unasked advice to a guest is cut too. *1002:* experts keep the cause and portable rules of thumb; history and procedure (hotline, payout days) go; asks stay, officials' answers go.
- **HKS-13. A party is cleared by evidence and other voices, never by his own pleading** (guess,
  1109, where the host himself is a party).
  *Full study of 1109 (2026-11-24):* other voices stay when they explain the host's motive or the law, not when they vouch for him; his own "I'm not involved" is cut. Candidate: about a person in custody, keep feelings and hedged readings ("ผมอาจจะคิดผิดก็ได้"), cut verdicts ("the dumbest criminal"), likely for legal safety.
- **HKS-14. The long form is the master; the shorts are selections from it** (seen 12/12,
  [study/shortform.md](study/shortform.md)). FB = the same hook + a digest of the whole YT in its
  order, ending on the hardest line; 1-min = the same hook + one beat. Why: one judgment of the
  story, reused, so every version tells it the same way.
  *Narrowed (1002 full study):* the FB groups one place's material together, jumping back in YT order once (FB 88.8–107.4), not only to hook lines.
- **HKS-15. The shorter the cut, the harder the ending** (seen 12/12). The YT may end calm; the FB
  ends on a defiant line, an accusation or a verdict; the 1-min ends on the punch or the shocking
  number. Why: a feed viewer decides to share on the last line.
  The user's own labels show it ([study/labels.md](study/labels.md)): every day that uses ORANGE
  ("ending sentences") ends the YT on it (7/7), and YELLOW ("must be in") makes up more of the 1-min
  than of the YT on 7 of 9 days: the labels are the first pass of this judgment.
- **HKS-16. Select by labelled topics before cutting** (seen on one day, 20260929, whose `topic
  cutting` sequence is the user's own rough topic draft of 2026-09-30, see [design-topic-cut.md](design-topic-cut.md)).
  The whole broadcast is razored at every topic change and each topic gets a colour; on that day the
  unlabelled topics (intro, ads, banter) never reach the cut, YELLOW is most of it, ORANGE ends it.
  n = 1: on 1123 the last three kept pieces are unlabelled, so "no colour = drop" is not general. Then the cut is trimmed from the kept topics.
  Why (guess): one fast pass decides *what*, so the slow pass only decides *how*. For drafts: produce
  this pass (topic boundaries + a label each) first; it is the user's own review format.
- **HKS-17. Dress the cut from the template kit, not by hand** (seen 12/12 + 0929 + EP.162/167,
  [study/template.md](study/template.md)). Every day uses the same nine pieces: the hook stack
  (dim 50, black tone + vignette, zoom, film damage, red-blue glitch, a 1–2 frame invert), the
  `SFX_โหนกระแส` sting in a short gap after the hook, the BGM bed on its 56.48 s loop grid, the end
  stack and `CG_END LOGO`; the hook's headline cards are After Effects Linked Comps. Film damage marks
  footage the show frames or lifts, not the studio.
- **HKS-18. A compilation is one theme, four equal cases** (seen EP.162, EP.167). The title card
  names the theme ("รวมเรื่องช้ำใจ", "รวมมหากาพย์บ้านพักคนชรา"); each case gets about the same time
  (19–24 % of its source); the hook is the single strongest confrontation; each case opens on its
  premise and ends on a button; the cases play in source order (HKS-C12, confirmed); the source runs on 2–5 s under the case card with only silence or the
  broadcast's goodbye under it (EP.162, words checked), never a cut-off line. Why (guess): a compilation is watched for a mood, so balance beats ranking.
  The title card is the episode's thumbnail animated in After Effects, same headline (seen EP.162's
  comp); the thumbnail and its headline PNGs already sit in the day folder before the cut (seen
  EP.168, n = 1; who makes them is not known). So: read the day's thumbnail before proposing a theme.
  Footage is fitted to the width (56.25 %, letterboxed) with blur boxes on the broadcast's logos
  (EP.162, EP.167). Every edge gets a glitch + a 1–2 frame Invert flash (EP.162, EP.167); the hook stack + film damage
  appear once, on EP.167's end tail, and not in EP.162. `CG BG` is blended (Screen), not an opaque card.
  Loudness (seen EP.167, n = 1): a source more than ~5 LU quieter than the rest is raised **+3 dB**,
  louder ones are left alone; the end music then sits ~3 LU under the last line.
  Cap (agent, EP.168): a boosted clip must stay at or under -1 dBTP. Nine of EP.168's 33 boosted clips
  had a 0 dBTP transient in a -20 LUFS source; capping them (most gain that fits, never below 0 dB) cut
  full-scale samples 2,588 → 91 (the rest: one source at its own level, SFX hits on talk). EP.167's own
  export has 1 such sample.
- **HKS-19. Three or four headline cards tell the whole arc** (seen 13/13: 3 After Effects cards on 1001
  and 1003, 4 elsewhere; [study/headlines.md](study/headlines.md)).
  Two lines each, a punch then the fact; setup → escalation → the worst detail or the turn → the
  climax or the twist; a number wherever the story has one; the other side's words in quotes. The
  voice under them plays only the emotional peak. Why (guess): the viewer gets the premise from the
  text in 15 s and stays for how it happened.
  *Narrowed (full studies 2026-11-24):* 1109's cards carry no number although the day has three; 1002's cards carry every number and the voice none; 1118's cards tell one guest's arc while the voice under them tells another's. Cards run 2–3 lines.

## Pass 2: cross-checks of every study (agent, 2026-11-24)

All 14 daily studies and the three compilation studies were read again against the rules above. These are
**candidates**: each was seen in two or more cuts, and none is confirmed by the user. Day lists name the
study files; the times are in each study.

New patterns:
- **HKS-20. The picture moves; the words stay in order.** A proof picture is taken from anywhere in the
  broadcast and laid under the claim it proves, even 100+ minutes away (seen 1012, 1020, 1110, 1117, 1120,
  1123). This is why HKS-5's word moves are rare: the picture moves instead.
- **HKS-21. The outgoing shot holds over the sound cut.** A 0.6–4.6 s patch of the shot before the join,
  or of the seconds around it, covers the jump (seen 1012, 1020, 1109, 1110, 1112, 1117, 1118).
  Covers under 4 s are normal (1001, 1003: half of 50 under 3 s).
- **HKS-22. The host's opening intro or recap is cut** (seen 1112, 1117, 1120, 1123; 1118 keeps 1 s of 192).
  The show's own process goes too: crew talk, break housekeeping, calls that never connect, "tomorrow"
  (1001, 1002, 1003). The online-only part after the TV part is used heavily (1001 ~620 s, 1003 ~700 s).
- **HKS-23. Feelings stay only if they move events.** Grief, pity and regret are cut unless they explain
  an action (seen 1020, 1109, 1110, 1117, 1118, 1120). A guest's own damaging admissions stay (1112,
  1120, 1123).
- **HKS-24. Keep the result, drop the working-out.** A live test or the host's arithmetic goes; its
  conclusion stays (seen 1117, 1118). A set-up stays only when a later line pays it off (1112, 1118, 1120).
