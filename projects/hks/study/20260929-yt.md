## The why (guess)

1. **The hook is the most counter-intuitive practical tip**, not the headline topic: everyone knows
   flooded cars are bad; few know that towing an EV the wrong way wrecks it. It is paid off late (870 s),
   in broadcast order, so the promise holds the viewer through the long middle.
2. **Advice days keep broadcast order** (one reorder in the body: the air-filter tip at 196 s, src
   540 → after 627–640, so the "dry it out" steps sit together). This fits HKS-11: the day's type
   sets the shape. A how-to has no plot to rearrange; order follows the expert's own logic.
3. **Word-level trimming of the experts** (seen: 136–143, 285–331, 541–568, 761–781, 895–918 run in
   0.3–2 s pieces with +0.2–2 s source gaps): hesitations and repeats are cut out of explanations,
   then covered by inserts on V2 (58 covers) with film damage on the inserts. Stories are cut by the
   sentence; explanations by the phrase.
4. **The same tip is told three times** ("don't start it": 90–100, 136–143, 677–680, 743–761). On an
   advice day repetition is the point, unlike story days (M-4 "each point once" does not hold here).
5. **The end is a social line, not a tip**: the cut closes on who gets hurt (people without
   insurance), the human angle of an explainer day (compare HKS on the ending: the last beat of the
   hook's promise, [README.md](README.md) point 11).
