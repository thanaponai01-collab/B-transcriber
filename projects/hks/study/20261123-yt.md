## What the user did and the why behind it

1. **The hook is two channels telling two things at once** (seen 0–17.1). Text cards tell the
   whole arc in four beats: *setup* "อุกอาจกลาง RCA! แม่ลูกขายดอกไม้ถูกอุ้มขึ้นรถตู้" → *demand*
   "เรียกค่าไถ่ 5 แสนบาท อ้างข้อหาค้ามนุษย์!" → *twist* "บังคับเซ็นเอกสารเปล่า เพื่อทำสัญญากู้เงินอำพราง"
   → *climax* "แก๊งผู้กำกับละคร บุกถึงบ้าน เหตุบานปลายแทงกันดับ!". The voice meanwhile plays the
   *accusation* ("they said I had a warrant… human trafficking", 3.4 s) and then the *climax*
   ("my brother took a knife and stabbed him", 14 s). Why (guess): the viewer gets the full
   premise in 17 s from the text and the emotional peak from the voice, so the curiosity gap is
   "how does a flower seller end up here", not "what is this about".
2. **The hook borrows lines the body plays again** (seen: src 237.5–240.9 at 0 s and 36.4 s;
   src 3329–3399 at 4.6 s and 868 s). M-4 (each point once) does not apply to the hook: the hook
   is a promise, the body is the delivery. *(guess; ask)*
3. **The body opens on the guest's first story sentence** ("หนูขายของอยู่กับลูกหนูที่ RCA หน้าร้าน
   Roots 66", 18.7 s); the stand-in host's 2-minute intro (S1.1) is gone. Rule 1, seen again.
4. **Factual questions are answered where the viewer asks them; framing waits until events are
   done** (seen). "Why 500k?" (asked 13 min later on air) is answered right after the 500k demand
   (285 s). "Who is ท่าน?" is answered right after the forced signature (639 s), as a reveal that
   lifts a long section. But the lawyer's law (3.2) goes *after* the stabbing (985 s) and "did you
   believe the warrant" (4.2) goes near the end (1091 s), although the warrant is first mentioned
   at 36 s. Tie-break (guess): a *fact* the viewer is missing moves to the question; an
   *interpretation* (law, blame) waits until the story is told.
5. **Each act ends on a pull into the next** (seen 719–757): the release is cut so it ends on
   "they followed us home on a motorbike… I called 191", the cue for the night of the 16th.
6. **Pace follows how visual the beat is** (seen). The abduction and the stabbing (on camera) are
   cut tight under CCTV (S3.1: 27 pieces in 55 s). The contract monologue (no footage of it) runs
   long and whole on the studio picture (402–639 s).
7. **A frame-held picture while someone names who is in it** (seen 792.8–812: the CCTV still with
   red boxes held 19 s while she names ท่าน and Big; 1036–1091 the police's 4-suspect graphic held
   while the host reads the names). The picture holds still exactly as long as the words point at
   it. When the host says "Big is the one in the blue shirt who grabbed them at RCA", the held
   graphic gives way to the RCA CCTV with Big circled (1050–1057), then the graphic comes back.
   Picture proves the words.
8. **A reorder join is covered** (seen 985.7: a 2 s CCTV clip over the −722 s source jump from
   the lawyer in 3.3 to the lawyer in 3.2). Same rule as rule 5: never show a jump.
9. **The end is reassurance, fast** (seen 1159–1189, 30 s): fear (3 still free) → quit selling →
   lawyer: "the 35k comes back, the 300k loan is void by law". The viewer leaves with the threat
   closed. (guess: the show's ending is the guest's safety and money, not the crime.)
10. **Film damage marks "found footage" the show extracted, not the broadcast's own full-screen
    pictures** (seen, partial): it is on the hook, the S3 house CCTV / phone video (757–917) and the
    warrant hold (1036–1091), but not on the RCA CCTV the broadcast aired full screen (27–175 s).
    This contradicts rule 6 "film damage on all B-roll". *(question for the user)*
11. **The 3HD bug stays on studio and broadcast footage** (seen in frames 20–700 s). Rule 6 says

## How I'd make it better (all guess; for the user to judge)

- **402–639 s, 3:57 of one voice on one studio shot pair.** It is the most unusual part (a
  kidnapping turned into a "loan"), but it is the longest stretch with no new picture and no
  question; a viewer is most likely to drop here, at the 7–10 min mark. Options: keep the host's
  "what number did it end at?" questions between parts (ask, then tell); cut the car
  power-of-attorney detail (607–639, it changes nothing later); show the contract if the broadcast
  aired a picture of it.
- **674–719 s host recap** repeats the forced signature the viewer heard 40 s earlier
  (607–639). Keep the money line (35k + 2.5 baht gold), drop the second telling of "they signed
  before I saw it": M-4.
- **The reveal at 639 s is the strongest turn after the stabbing** and it is spent mid-act.
  Ending the money act on it ("…he said he worked in the palace. — He's a drama director.") and
  cutting the host recap after it would land the act on a twist instead of a summary.
- **4.2 (1091–1159)** is a 68 s single piece where the lawyer says the mother may share blame for
  bringing her child to sell flowers. It reopens doubt about the victim just before the ending.
  Either cut to the guest's answer ("I believed it because they named my friend Da") or move the
  blame part out: as placed, it softens the ending.
- **Ending:** "3 men are still free" (1166 s) is the open loop a follow-up episode can use; the cut
  closes on the loan being void, which is calmer but less of a reason to come back. Keep as is if
  the channel prefers closure (ask).
