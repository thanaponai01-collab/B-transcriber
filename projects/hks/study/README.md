then a synthesis below. **Nothing here is confirmed:** **seen** = read from the user's cut,
**guess** = the agent's inference. A point becomes a rule or principle only after the user answers
the questions at the end.

`study_episode.py` (kept in `output/hks_study/` with its outputs: `<day>.txt` story skeleton,
`<day>.pieces.txt` every kept piece with its words). 20261123 was also read live in Premiere with 21

What the numbers say (seen):
- **The length is fixed, the source is not.** Every show is 16.9–19.9 min, from 40 to 158 min of
  broadcast. The kept share (12–46 %) follows from the length, not the other way round: the
  target length decides how hard each episode is cut.
- **The hook is 18–27 s, every time.**
- **The body starts 2–5 min into the broadcast** (just after the opening talk) **and reaches the
  broadcast's last minutes**: the cut covers the whole show and thins it, rather than picking one
  stretch.
- **Pace is a per-episode choice:** 5.7–15.7 cuts per minute, cover share 7–35 %. It follows how
  much footage the story has (see each episode file).
- Reorders are rare (0–4 per cut): broadcast order is the default; moving material is the
  exception and each case has a reason (see the episode files).

*20261112's footage is five files; the script read one, so its source length and kept share are wrong.

## Synthesis: what holds across episodes (the why)

Nine daily cuts plus the EP.167 compilation. Each point names the episodes it was seen in; the why is a
**guess** until the user answers. The candidate principles are numbered `HKS-n` in
[../principles.md](../principles.md).

1. **One length, any story** (seen 12/12). 17–20 min whatever the broadcast. The length budget decides
   how hard the cut is, so whole topics go first, then retellings, then words.
2. **The hook is the promise, and the body keeps it** (seen 9/9). 18–27 s; title cards carry the facts,
   the voice carries the ordeal or the clash (1123, 1003, 1020). Hook lines play again in the body
   (1123, 1003, 1112, 1117), and the hook's last beat often comes back as the ending (1112, 1117).
   Where the hook asks a question the body never answers, the cut is weaker (1020: "is it real?" is
   answered on air, as a cow's eye, but cut).
3. **One spine, proved** (seen 1012, 1112, 1120, 1001). The thread with proof, a money trail or a
   confrontation carries the cut; side stories go whole, even big ones (1012 drops the biggest
   victim; 1112 drops where the 31 million came from).
4. **The clearest telling wins, from anyone** (seen 1001, 1020, 1112, 1120). A host's one-line recap
   can replace the guest's slow version; the first telling beats the later recaps; a point comes
   back only with new evidence (1012).
5. **Broadcast order is the default; a move needs a reason** (seen: 0 reorders in 1001, 1112, 1117,
   1120). The reasons seen:
   - a fact answered where the viewer asks for it (1123 "why 500k", 1003 the daughter's letter);
   - evidence before the denial, so it plays as a trial (1003);
   - a scattered thread gathered into one act (1110 the wardrobe, 1020 the money);
   - a section ended on its number or verdict (1012).
   When the broadcast already ran in story order, nothing moves.
6. **Let the accused answer, at length** (seen 1003, 1020, 1120, 167; 1120's other side is the
   longest piece in the cut). Material that hurts the guest stays (1120). Exception: 1003 cut the
   son's side.
7. **Cut hard only under a picture that proves the words** (seen 9/9). Stills, lists, chats, CCTV,
   price boards land on the words that point at them; under them the sound is cut to the bone
   (12–30 pieces in 20–45 s). Where there is nothing to show, pieces run long and whole. This is
   also why pace varies per episode: footage-rich stories are dense (1117 15.7 cuts/min), talk-only
   ones are slow (1012 5.7).
8. **The host stays when he carries the story** (seen in every episode, against rule 3 as written).
   Kept: the question that opens a chapter (1110, 1117), the challenge the viewer wants asked (1120),
   the clearest summary (1001, 1020), the verdict or the episode's point (1003, 1012, 167), legal
   cover ("เราไม่ได้กล่าวหาใคร", 1117). Cut: warm-up, clarifying, name checks, filler, most jokes.
9. **Viewer comments: cut, unless they are the objection someone answers** (seen 1001, 1110 keep one;
   1123, 1120 cut them).
10. **Law follows the fact it explains** (seen 1112 early, right after the fact; 1123, 1012, 1020 late,
    as the verdict; 1110 law is the subject). Law kept as a lecture of its own is dropped (1120's
    Supreme Court talk). The law that stays answers "what happens now?".
11. **The ending is the last beat of the hook's promise, not a fixed mood** (seen: reassurance 1123,
    resolve 1020, outrage 1003 and 167, open threat 1112 and 1117, the guest's own closing words 1120).
12. **Each case ends on a button line** in a compilation (167: stakes, apology, outrage); the host's
    verdict stays there because a recap needs a judgment.

13. **Holdout test** ([holdout/README.md](holdout/README.md)): blind predictions on 1002, 1109 and 1118
    held on length, hook-plays-out, broadcast order and host-as-questioner. They narrowed "one spine"
    (story days only), "law" (only as the answer to a guest's own case) and "ending". They added
    HKS-11 (the day's type sets the shape), HKS-12 and HKS-13. The hook's exact lines and the single
    reorder are where the list predicts worst.
14. **The BGM bed loops on a fixed 56.48 s grid** (measured on all 13 daily cuts, 1001–1123 and 0929:
    every loop-to-loop gap is 56.48 s except one deliberate 25.84 s loop on 1002; the compilations
    EP.162 / EP.167 have no bed, only the tail at the gap and the end): each new loop
    of `[BGM]Where Everything Began` starts 56.48 s into the previous one, where the old loop's music
    ends (−11 dB at 56.5 s) and just before its fade, so the bed never dips (level measured; the beat
    alignment is a guess). The agent's own fix (guess, not seen from the user): when a cut shortens
    the bed, shorten one loop from its head and keep the 56.48 grid after it; never stop a loop
    mid-music ([20261123-yt.md](20261123-yt.md), v6 music). It is the template's own grid ([template.md](template.md)). EP.167 uses the same file only as a −12 dB
    tail under the post-hook sting and the end.

15. **Selection is a labelled topic pass before the cut** (seen on one day, 20260929; the pass is the
    user's own draft, [../design-topic-cut.md](../design-topic-cut.md)): the broadcast is razored at every topic
    change and each topic labelled; there, unlabelled topics (intro, ads, banter) never reach the cut, YELLOW topics make most of it, the ORANGE topic ends it. A draft that reproduces this pass
    (topic boundaries + a label each) reproduces the user's selection step ([20260929-yt.md](20260929-yt.md)).
16. **Recipes come from one template kit** ([template.md](template.md)): the hook stack, the sting, the
    BGM grid and the end logo are on all 12 days; the headline cards are After Effects Linked Comps.

17. **A compilation sells a theme** (seen EP.162, EP.167): an After Effects title card names the
    feeling or saga, four cases share it with equal time (about 20 % of each source), the hook is one
    case's peak, each case ends on a button and the source runs on under the `CG BG` card. Order
    follows the story (part 1 before part 2), not the file numbers.

18. **The headline cards carry the plot in four steps** ([headlines.md](headlines.md), 13 days): two
    lines a card (a punch, then the fact), setup → escalation → worst detail or turn → climax or twist,
    a number on almost every day, the accused's words in quotes.

## Where the rules and the cuts disagree (for the user)

**Answered 2026-11-24** (all but point 7, which the user is not sure of): see HKS-C1 to C12 in
[../principles.md](../principles.md) and the rewritten rules 1–6 in [../rules.md](../rules.md).

Many contradictions are older cuts made before the 2026-11-23 rules (covers ≥ 4 s, not one frame of
jump). The rest are real questions:

1. **Rule 1** (open on the guest's first story sentence): 1110 and 1117 open on a host question, 1020
   on her background. Is "ask, then tell" an allowed opening?
2. **Rule 3** (cut host opinions): see point 8 above. Rewrite as "keep the host when he asks the
   chapter question, challenges, sums up best, or gives the verdict"?
3. **Rule 4** (always cut viewer comments): see point 9.
4. **Rule 2** (drop legal analysis): see point 10. Rewrite as "law right after the fact it explains, or
   as the verdict; never as a lecture"?
5. **Rule 6** (film damage on all B-roll): it is on some covers and not others in every episode, and no
   pattern fits all of them (not zoomed-only, not long-only). What decides it?
6. **Covers ≥ 4 s**: half the covers in October cuts are 0.6–3 s. Is 4 s the standard from now on, or
   only for cutaways and B-roll (not a held outgoing shot)?
7. **1012 951–967** plays 16.6 s with no cover that the sponsor pass marked as an ad (A-TECH). Its
   words ("วงข้าวเปลือกแสดงสดที่ บริษัท A-TECH… หมู", mostly music, right before the pork argument)
   suggest a video of the event the show played as evidence, not an ad (the sponsor pass also
   mislabels story in 1003). Check by eye.
8. **3HD bug and the TV clock**: visible on studio frames in 1123 and in two 1110 hold stills. Fine on
   studio and broadcast pictures?

## Recurring ways to do better (guess; each is timecoded in its episode file)

- **A promise ledger.** Every question the hook, a title card or the host raises gets an answer in the
  cut, or is left open on purpose. Misses seen: 1020 "is it real?", 1110 the WatchDog question
  (answered 15 min later), 1110 "why is Bang Phlat slow" with no setup, 1012 the money never repaid,
  1117 the premise (fell, died) not said until 5:27.
- **Watch the middle.** 1120 (8–14 min) and 1123 (7–10 min) both run 4–6 min with no new picture and
  no question. Put a host question or an evidence picture in, or cut the side detail there.
- **Keep the strongest line the guest has.** The owner's "20,000 a month, a bucket" (1003), Tang's "if
  only I'd picked up" (1117) and the witness's points in the guest's favour (1120) were cut. Before
  locking, check each side's single strongest line.
