# HKS paper edit: rules and do-confirm

The 55 active `paper_edit` rules in [the registry](../../../projects/hks/registry/rules/). A gate id names the `hks_gates` check that measures the rule.

- **HKS-1** **One length, any story.** 17-20 min whatever the broadcast length; the budget decides how hard each episode is cut. Weakened: compilations differ (14:38-15:12).
- **HKS-2** **The hook is a promise the body plays out.** 18-27 s; its lines come back in the body. Which lines the user picks is the least predictable part. 1109: 13 of 14 hook pieces play again in the body. Weakened: a 17.3 s hook on EP.162.
- **HKS-3** Story days have one proven spine (narrowed: issue days and panels keep every voice, thinned; see HKS-11). Why: a thread with proof can't be argued away.
- **HKS-4** The first clear telling wins; a recap keeps only what's new; a host one-liner can replace a guest's slow version. Weakened: the better telling wins, not always the first (1001 T7, 1120, 1117). HKS-12 overrides HKS-4 (1118). (human only)
- **HKS-5** Broadcast order unless the viewer needs it elsewhere (0-4 moves per cut): a fact answered where it's asked, evidence before a denial, a scattered thread gathered.
- **HKS-10** The ending closes the lead thread (narrowed: the hook-echo ending is seen only in 1112 and 1117). The mood varies. Weakened: several cuts end open (1002, 1003, 1012, 1110).
- **HKS-11** **The day's type sets the shape.** Story day: one spine. Issue day: a briefing (the damage, each voice's best beat, the takeaways, the threat). Panel: every guest, thinned. Advice day: broadcast order with one move, experts trimmed word by word, the key tip repeated on purpose (M-4 does not apply), the hook is the most counter-intuitive tip, the end is the human cost.
- **HKS-13** **A party is cleared by evidence, never his own pleading.** A party is cleared by evidence and other voices, never by his own pleading (1109). Other voices stay when they explain the host's motive or the law, not when they vouch; about a person in custody, keep feelings and hedged readings, cut verdicts.
- **HKS-14** **The long form is the master; shorts are selections.** FB = the same hook + a digest of the whole YT in its order, ending on the hardest line; 1-min = the same hook + one beat. Narrowed: the FB groups one place's material together. Weakened: compilations have no YT long form (the FB D1 is the master).
- **HKS-15** **The shorter the cut, the harder the ending.** The YT may end calm; the FB ends on a defiant line, an accusation or a verdict; the 1-min ends on the punch or the shocking number. Every day that uses ORANGE ends the YT on it; YELLOW makes up more of the 1-min than of the YT. (human only)
- **HKS-18** **A compilation is one theme, four equal cases.** The title card names the theme; each case gets about the same time (19-24 % of its source); the hook is the single strongest confrontation; each case opens on its premise and ends on a button; cases in source order (HKS-C12); 2-5 s of source under the case card. Footage fitted to the width with blur boxes on logos; glitch + Invert at every edge; quieter sources (> ~5 LU) raised +3 dB, capped at -1 dBTP (agent, EP.168).
- **HKS-19** **Three or four headline cards tell the whole arc.** Two lines each, a punch then the fact; setup -> escalation -> the worst detail or the turn -> the climax or the twist; a number wherever the story has one; the other side's words in quotes. The voice under them plays only the emotional peak. Narrowed/weakened: some days carry no number; cards run 2-3 lines; on advice and issue days the voice plays a practical threat.
- **HKS-22** The host's opening intro or recap is cut; the show's own process goes too (crew talk, break housekeeping, calls that never connect, "tomorrow"). The online-only part after the TV part is used heavily.
- **HKS-23** **Feelings stay only if they move events.** Grief, pity and regret are cut unless they explain an action. A guest's own damaging admissions stay. (human only)
- **HKS-24** **Keep the result, drop the working-out.** A live test or the host's arithmetic goes; its conclusion stays. A set-up stays only when a later line pays it off.
- **HKS-25** **A verdict waits until the evidence is in.** The host's interpretation is moved after the facts.
- **HKS-26** **The hook's voice depends on the day type.** On issue and explainer days the host or official carries the hook with a warning or tip; on story days, the victim or the clash. Hook lines often come from late in the broadcast.
- **HKS-C4** **Host's on-air intro and recap are cut.** The host's on-air intro and recap are cut (HKS-22) because the hook does the intro's job and a recap repeats what the viewer just saw, and for length.
- **HKS-C5** **Viewer comments and host warm-up opinions are cut.** Viewer comments and the host's warm-up opinions are cut because they slow the story and for neutrality. Exception: a viewer comment that is the objection a guest or the host then answers.
- **HKS-C6** Ask, then tell is an allowed opening (1110, 1117): the story may open on the host question that sets up the guest's first story sentence.
- **HKS-C7** The host stays when he carries the story (HKS-8 confirmed, rule 3 rewritten): the chapter question, the challenge the viewer wants asked, the clearest summary, the verdict, legal cover. Cut his warm-up, clarifying questions, name checks, filler and most jokes.
- **HKS-C8** Law answers the guest's own case, never lectures (HKS-12 confirmed, rule 2 rewritten): right after the fact it explains, or as the verdict. The user: "sometimes it could be somewhere at around ending like every story tells, then law answers".
- **HKS-C11** Headline cards mainly pay off in the body; a card may tease beyond it, but the default is a payoff.
- **HKS-C12** A compilation plays its cases in source order: by the number the user gave each downloaded file; with no number, by the YouTube posting date. A story told across several videos stays in its own order.
- **HKS-C15** **Each side's strongest line is in.** Before locking, name each side's single strongest line and check it is kept.
- **HKS-C16** **An open ending says it is open.** If the story is unresolved, end on a line that says so (a threat, a pending case, a follow-up), not on a cut that just stops.
- **HKS-C18** **Watch time counts, but partly.** Each second is judged by whether it keeps the viewer watching, after honesty and fairness: the accused's answer (HKS-6), legal cover and the facts that keep the story true stay even when slow. (human only)
- **HKS-C21** **The other side's rebuttal stays.** When a guest accuses, the other side's rebuttal stays if the broadcast has it. Narrows HKS-6 to a rule.
- **HKS-C22** A fact the picture shows need not be said; the check is then that the picture really shows it (in 1123 the graphic marks the boss "ออกหมายจับ", not "จับแล้ว").
- **HKS-C23** A punchline needs its set-up (HKS-28 confirmed). The user put the bet's condition ("500 ล้านพี่หนุ่มเปิดผมตาม") in front of the payoff ("ผมโกนรอเลยพี่"). Same idea as ask-then-tell (HKS-C6).
- **HKS-C24** **The body doesn't replay a hook clip mid-body.** The body doesn't replay a hook clip in the middle; the ending may, as the payoff (HKS-29 confirmed). The hook promises; the ending pays.
- **HKS-C27** **Tell the story to a first-time viewer.** Long form gives the viewer the set-up (who, where, what happened) and a taste of the event before the speeches that need it. "Get in fast" (HKS-R1) means no warm-up. It does not mean no context.
- **HKS-R1** Get into each story fast: the guest's first story sentence, or the host's summary if the guest is slow, or the host question that sets it up (ask, then tell; HKS-C6, 2026-11-24).
- **HKS-R2** **Each point told once.** (M-4) Each point told once. Drop retellings, off-topic asides, backstory and feelings that don't move events (shorten as much as possible). Law stays only as the answer to the guest's own case: right after the fact it explains, or as the verdict, often near the end once the story is told; never a lecture (HKS-C8, 2026-11-24).
- **HKS-R3** Keep the host when he carries the story: the chapter question, the challenge the viewer wants asked, the clearest summary, the verdict, legal cover. Cut his warm-up, clarifying questions, name checks, filler, most jokes and his on-air intro and recap (HKS-C4, C7, 2026-11-24). Keep a host connector to bridge pieces that don't join. Host reactions may be added for drama.
- **HKS-R4** **Viewer comments and sponsors cut.** Viewer comments read on air: cut, unless one is the objection a guest or the host then answers (HKS-C5, 2026-11-24). Sponsors: always cut.
- **HKS-X1** **The story is the order, not the broadcast.** Material moves to where the viewer needs it: the "why 500k" answer right where the number comes up, the voice recording right after "it was still 500,000" as proof, the host's recap of what was paid at the end of a story part, the lawyer's legal framing after the events it explains (events first, law second).
- **HKS-X5** **Ask, then tell.** Keep the host question that sets up the next beat ("and when you got home, what happened?"); it keeps the viewer tracking what happens next.
- **HKS-X7** **Keep the law short and late; end fast.** Keep the law short and late: the lawyer stays where it explains what happened, not as a separate lecture. End fast: the last three topics take 31 s.
- **HKS-X15** 2-3 hook options, each built a different way (the clash, the shocking fact, the question). The user picks; record the pick and the user's reason in `memory/`.
- **HKS-X25** Hook button = set-up + payoff (HKS-C23). Before picking a hook's last line, read the line before it in the source; if the payoff needs it, take both.
- **HKS-X26** **Short body: no mid-body hook clip.** Short body: no mid-body clip that's already in the hook (HKS-C24). Check the source ranges of every body piece against the hook's; only the ending may overlap, and then with more than the hook showed.
- **HKS-X39** Tell it like a film to someone seeing it the first time. Before the guest's detail, give the viewer a taste of the context (who these people are, where, what happened between them), then go into the story. Never open a story part on a line that only makes sense if you already know the case. Rule 1's "get in fast" means no warm-up, not no context.
- **HKS-X71** **I built the story with no context first.** Before building, write the story's set-up in two lines (who, where, what happened) and check that the cut gives the viewer those facts before the first detail that needs them.
- **HKS-X80** **Two programs.** โหนกระแส is the main show, cut from the raw broadcast. โหนหลังกระแส is a compilation of usually 4 already-edited โหนกระแส long videos, cut down again. Rules read from the dailies are about cutting a broadcast; rules from EP.162/167 (HKS-18 on) are about re-cutting finished cuts (the longer-pieces reason is an inference, not yet asked).
- **HKS-X89** Set-up in two lines: who these people are, where (the houses, the shop), what happened between them. The cut gives a first-time viewer these facts before the first detail that needs them.
- **HKS-X90** Give a taste of the event itself (the clip, its sound) before the people talk about it. If the broadcast plays footage of the incident, the body uses it where the story reaches that moment.
- **HKS-X91** Story order, not the broadcast's order. Each story part answers "what happens next?" for someone who has never seen the case. Cut the back-and-forth and the repeats, but keep the set-up.
- **HKS-X98** **Set-up is not backstory.** I read "get in fast" and "drop backstory" as "cut the context". The where and the incident clip are set-up, not backstory.
- **HKS-X109** ****Tighten first, drop story last.**.** Meet the length by tightening before dropping any story passage; drop a passage only when the tightened cut is still long, and say what fact it loses. On 20261126 the agent dropped 7 passages (3.6 min) for the 20-min line; the user kept three of them (the ธรรมนัส clause, คุณกิ๊ก's own donation, the face-scan case) and made the time from 360 s of pauses and filler. Confirmed by the user 2026-11-26 ("Yes, confirm both").
- **HKS-X111** **A film-promo day feels fun, not like an ad.** On a film-promo episode the long form feels fun, not like an advertisement for the film (user, 20261124: the v3 cut read like an ad). Not every part can be fun: keep the story that works and add the fun moments in, whole (the user, on the v4 fun paper edit).
- **HKS-X112** **The piece opens on the line the user names.** When the user names the line a piece opens on, it opens on that line and everything before it goes (user, 20261124, on the highlight: "start with this ... remove all in front").
- **HKS-X114** **A talking insert is not a body piece.** An interview clip the broadcast inserts, where someone talks to camera (the DSI interview of 20261126), is not a body piece: the user dropped both places the agent used it (1:25:16, 1:27:48).
- **M-1** **Learn the why, not presets.** Every observed choice is recorded as the intent behind it (viewer effect, constraint, story role). Values (109%, -12 dB, Posterize 0.2) are today's example, not a setting to replay.
- **M-4** Each point is told once (confirmed in both projects); HKS cut rule 2 holds the user's words.

## Do–confirm

Each question needs a written answer in the handover, keyed by its rule id.

1. **HKS-1** How long is the cut, against the 17-20 min norm (or the compilation norm)?
2. **HKS-2** Where does each hook line come back in the body?
3. **HKS-3** What is the spine, and which proof holds it?
4. **HKS-5** Which moves were made, and why does the viewer need each?
5. **HKS-10** Which thread does the ending close, or is it open on purpose?
6. **HKS-11** Which day type is this, and which shape does the cut follow?
7. **HKS-13** Where a party is cleared, by which evidence or voice, and is any self-pleading left?
8. **HKS-14** Is every short piece taken from the long form, in its order?
9. **HKS-18** What share of its source does each case keep, and does each open on its premise and end on a button?
10. **HKS-19** How many cards, and what arc does each step tell?
11. **HKS-22** Are the host's intro, recap and the show's process talk out?
12. **HKS-24** Which working-out was dropped, and does each kept set-up have its payoff?
13. **HKS-25** Does each verdict come after the evidence it rests on (timecodes)?
14. **HKS-26** Whose voice carries the hook, and does it fit the day type?
15. **HKS-C4** Are the host's on-air intro and recap out of the cut?
16. **HKS-C5** Is any viewer comment or host warm-up opinion left, and if so which answer follows it?
17. **HKS-C6** Where a story opens on a host question, does the guest's first story sentence follow it?
18. **HKS-C7** For each kept host line, which carrying role does it play?
19. **HKS-C8** Which guest question does each law passage answer, and where is it?
20. **HKS-C11** Where in the body does each headline card pay off (timecode), or which one teases on purpose?
21. **HKS-C12** In what order do the cases play, and does it match the file numbers or posting dates?
22. **HKS-C15** What is each side's strongest line, and its timecode in the cut?
23. **HKS-C16** Is the story resolved; if not, which last line says it is open?
24. **HKS-C21** For each accusation, is the other side's rebuttal in (timecode), or does the broadcast lack one?
25. **HKS-C22** For each fact left unsaid, which picture shows it (timecode), and does it say exactly that?
26. **HKS-C23** For each punchline, is its set-up line in front of it?
27. **HKS-C24** Does any mid-body piece replay a hook clip?
28. **HKS-C27** What set-up facts and event clip does the viewer get before the first speech that needs them?
29. **HKS-R1** What does each story part open on (timecode and first line), and is it the guest's first story sentence, the host's summary or the set-up question?
30. **HKS-R2** Which retellings, asides and backstory were dropped, and where (timecode) does law appear as the answer to a guest's own case?
31. **HKS-R3** For each kept host line, which role does it play (chapter question, challenge, summary, verdict, legal cover, connector)?
32. **HKS-R4** Is any viewer comment or sponsor left in the cut, and for each kept comment, which answer follows it?
33. **HKS-X1** Which pieces were moved out of broadcast order, and why does the viewer need each where it now sits?
34. **HKS-X5** Which host set-up questions were kept before their beats (timecodes)?
35. **HKS-X7** How many seconds of law or expert talk are in the cut and where, and how long do the last topics run?
36. **HKS-X15** Which hooks were offered and how is each built; which did the user pick, and where is the reason recorded?
37. **HKS-X25** What is the hook's last line, and does it need the line before it in the source?
38. **HKS-X26** Does any body piece's source range overlap the hook's, other than the ending?
39. **HKS-X39** Write the set-up (who, where, what happened) and the timecode where the cut gives each fact.
40. **HKS-X71** What is the two-line set-up, and where does the cut give each fact before the detail that needs it?
41. **HKS-X80** Is this a daily or a compilation, and which rule set does the cut follow?
42. **HKS-X89** What are the two set-up lines, and where does the cut give each fact?
43. **HKS-X90** Where does the incident footage play, before the talk about it and where the story reaches it?
44. **HKS-X91** What question does each story part answer next, in order?
45. **HKS-X98** Which set-up (the where, the incident clip) is kept, separate from the backstory dropped?
46. **HKS-X109** How many seconds did tightening take out, and which story passages were still dropped for length (source timecodes, the fact each loses)?
47. **HKS-X111** Which fun moments are in, and does any part read like an ad for the film?
48. **HKS-X112** Did the user name an opening line, and does the piece open on it?
49. **HKS-X114** Is any body piece an inserted interview clip, and why does it stay?
50. **M-1** Which values in the build were measured for this episode rather than reused from an earlier one?
51. **M-4** Is any point told twice in the cut (timecodes)?
